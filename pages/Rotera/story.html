How Rotera was made

Many teams still build their shift schedule in a group chat, on a sheet of paper or in a spreadsheet. Rotera replaces that: staff say when they can work, the manager builds the week, and everyone sees who is really working.

One codebase, iPhone and browser

Rotera is written once with React Native and Expo, and runs both as an iPhone app and as a web app in the browser. There is no Android app: Android users open the web app and add it to their home screen.

Every organization kept apart

Many companies use the same Rotera. Their data is separated in the database itself with Supabase Row-Level Security, not only in the app's code, so one team can never see another team's schedule.

Wishes that say what people mean

The heart of Rotera is wishes. In 1.1 each day was morning, afternoon, either or off. In 1.2 people could wish for their own hours, like from 17:00, or link days in a choice, like Monday or Tuesday, and the draft built itself every time wishes came in.

In 1.3 managers got the week back in their own hands: that day's wishes sit right under the schedule, and one tap adds a person to a shift. The self-building draft is still there as a switch.

Covers and trades with a trail

A request for cover or a shift trade happens in the app, goes to the manager for approval, and the schedule updates for everyone. Rotera is available in English, Slovenian and German.
