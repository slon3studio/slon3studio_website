How PayDay was made

Most hour trackers assume one employer and a fixed salary. PayDay assumes the opposite: a bar on weekends, a salon on Tuesdays, deliveries when they come up, each with its own rate, hours and tips.

Download on theApp Store
OverviewSupportPrivacy PolicyStory

Three questions

PayDay is built around three questions. What have I worked this month? What will the month pay me? And did the payslip actually match? Every screen exists to answer one of them.

A tab for every job

Each job gets its own tab, with its own rate, usual hours, colour and icon. The colour runs through the whole tab, so you always know which job you're looking at.

Not everyone is paid by the hour

A hairdresser is paid for the cut, not for the forty minutes, and so is a nail technician or a masseur. So a job is either paid by the hour, or per appointment with a price list, and the app speaks of shifts to one and appointments to the other.

A forecast you can trust

The month's projection keeps what you worked, what you planned and what is only an estimate clearly apart. Planned shifts never count as money earned, and booked days off no longer drag the forecast down.

Everything is stored on the phone with SwiftData. Sync between devices uses your own iCloud, only if you want it, and there is no account and no sign-up.

Fast releases

PayDay went from its first release to version 1.3 in three weeks, adding planning, four languages, per-appointment jobs and days off along the way.
