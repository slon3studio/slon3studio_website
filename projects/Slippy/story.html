How Slippy was made

At the end of a shift, waiters and cashiers add up a pile of card slips and receipts. Typing them in by hand is slow, and it's easy to slip up. Slippy reads them with the camera instead.

Not just the biggest number

A slip is full of numbers: dates, VAT, subtotals, card digits. Slippy doesn't simply take the biggest one. It scores every candidate by keywords for the total in several languages, like SKUPAJ, TOTAL, UKUPNO, BETRAG or IMPORTO, by currency and by where the line sits, and it drops dates, percentages, VAT and subtotals. It also fixes typical reading mistakes on crumpled or dirty slips.

Steady before it counts

An amount is only added once the same number is read in several frames in a row, with a tap of haptic feedback. The next slip is accepted after you move the last one away, so two slips with the same amount both count.

Exact sums

The calculator uses its own parser on exact decimal numbers, so there are none of the rounding errors that ordinary floating-point maths can cause with money.

Nothing leaves the phone

Text recognition runs entirely on the iPhone with Apple's Vision framework. Pictures of slips are never stored or sent anywhere, and Slippy works without an internet connection.
